Researcher/Coordinator

Yamagami Lab

2024 – 2025
Houston, TX

During my final year of undergrad, I worked on three studies focused on using biosignals to recognize gestures, identify exercises, and estimate physical exertion. Since two of these studies were early-stage, a big part of the work was deciding how to design the experiments and what data was worth collecting. For these studies, I designed and wrote protocols, collected data during participant sessions, and integrated smartwatches, motion capture systems, and EMG sensors into data collection pipelines. For the third study, I cleaned and processed EMG and IMU signals to demonstrate the cross-day stability of a novel gesture recognizer.

Teaching Assistant

Rice University - BIOS 385: Mechanisms of Neurons

Fall 2024
Houston, TX

After taking the course the previous year, I came back as a TA to run discussion sections for a subset of the course's students. We spent our time unpacking primary research articles by talking through figures, experimental design, and how each paper tied into key ideas of the course. Topics included persistent sodium currents in apteronotids (a slightly electric fish), the cAMP/PKA signaling pathway, short/long term habituation in zebrafish, and optogenetics. It was great teaching practice: I prepped slides each week, dug into readings and figures, and worked on walking students through each paper’s story in a way that felt more like conversation than lecture.

MRI Research Assistant

UTHealth Science Center

2023 – 2024
Houston, TX

Over four months as an MRI research assistant, I carved out a research project looking at brain scans from preterm children to explore possible neural links to developmental delays. I used neuroimaging tools like iBEAT and FSL’s FEAT and DTI pipelines, which meant spending a lot of time flipping between colorful brain slices on my screen and equally colorful research papers. Along the way, I built a Python pipeline to check if we had the right imaging and behavioral data, calculate behavior scores, and wrangle MRI data into a usable format. It all came together in a paper I presented to the director of the neuroscience program at Rice (basically the research equivalent of showing your parents a macaroni necklace).

Achievements:
